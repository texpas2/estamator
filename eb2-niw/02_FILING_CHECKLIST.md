# 02 — Filing Checklist & Mechanics

> Every fee, address, form edition and processing time below is marked
> `{{VERIFY}}` because USCIS changes them. Confirm each at **uscis.gov** on the
> day you file. Filing with a superseded form edition gets the package rejected
> and returned.

---

## A. Forms

| Form | Needed? | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **I-140**, Immigrant Petition for Alien Worker | **Yes — required** | Part 2, check the box for *"A member of the professions holding an advanced degree or an alien of exceptional ability … seeking a National Interest Waiver."* Part 1 (employer info): as a self-petitioner you enter **your own** information. |
| **ETA-9089** (PERM labor certification) | **No** | This is exactly what the NIW waives. Do not attach one. |
| **I-907**, Request for Premium Processing | Optional | Available for NIW I-140. `{{VERIFY}}` fee and clock (45 business days for this classification). Premium processing buys **speed, not approval** — and it gives the officer less time to be persuaded by a thin record. Only use it if your record is strong or your status is time-critical. |
| **I-485**, Adjustment of Status | Only if in the U.S. **and** your priority date is current | Can be filed concurrently with I-140 when current. Check the Visa Bulletin for your `{{COUNTRY_OF_CITIZENSHIP}}`. |
| **I-765** (EAD) / **I-131** (Advance Parole) | Only with I-485 | Usually filed together with I-485; typically no separate fee when filed with a pending I-485 `{{VERIFY}}`. |
| **I-824 / DS-260** | Only for consular processing | If you are outside the U.S., after I-140 approval the case goes to the National Visa Center for immigrant visa processing. |
| **G-1145**, e-Notification | Optional | Gives you a text/email receipt notice. |
| **G-28** | Only if represented | Attorney/accredited-representative appearance form. |

Pay by a single check/money order per form, or Form G-1450 for card. **Never one
combined check across forms** — USCIS will reject it.

## C. Where to file — `{{VERIFY}}`

USCIS publishes "Direct Filing Addresses for Form I-140." Standalone NIW
I-140s and concurrent I-140/I-485 filings go to **different** lockbox
addresses. Look it up the week you file; do not rely on an address from a
forum post.

## D. Supporting documents that must be in the package

- [ ] Cover letter with table of contents (`03_PETITION_LETTER.md` front matter)
- [ ] Petition letter / legal brief (`03`)
- [ ] Proposed endeavor statement (`04`)
- [ ] Personal statement, signed (`05`)
- [ ] Five-year plan (`06`)
- [ ] 5–7 recommendation letters, **signed on letterhead**, with each
      recommender's CV/bio attached (`07/`)
- [ ] Degree, transcripts, credential evaluation (`11`)
- [ ] Employment verification letters (`10`)
- [ ] Licenses and certifications (P.E., AACE CEP/CCP, PMP)
- [ ] Project evidence: close-out reports, estimate basis documents, org charts,
      award letters, redacted contract pages showing your role
- [ ] Independent national-importance evidence: DOE/EIA/BLS/GAO reports, trade
      press, industry association data (`09`)
- [ ] Passport biographic page, current visa, I-94, prior approval notices
- [ ] Certified English translations of every non-English document
      (translator's certificate of competence attached to each)
- [ ] Copy of every prior I-140/I-485 receipt or approval notice

## E. Assembly rules that actually get petitions read

1. **Tab everything.** Number exhibits sequentially and cite them in the brief as
   *(Exhibit 12)*. An officer who cannot find your proof treats it as absent.
2. **Two-hole punch at the top**, single-sided, no staples through text, no
   binders, no plastic sleeves.
3. **Put the strongest evidence first** within each prong. Officers read the
   brief and spot-check exhibits; they do not read 900 pages.
4. **Keep it to ~150–350 pages.** A 1,200-page package signals padding, and
   padding signals weakness.
5. **Redact** third-party confidential commercial terms from project documents,
   and say in the brief that you redacted them and why. Do not file anything
   your employer's confidentiality agreement forbids you to disclose — get
   written permission for project documents. This is a real risk in EPC work.
6. Keep a **complete scanned copy** of everything you file.

## F. After filing

| Event | What to do |
|---|---|
| Receipt notice (I-797C) | Your **priority date** is the filing date. Record it. |
| RFE (Request for Evidence) | You get a deadline, typically ~87 days. Do not file a partial response. See `12_RFE_RISK_ASSESSMENT.md` — it pre-answers the likely RFE. |
| NOID (Notice of Intent to Deny) | More serious. Get an attorney immediately. |
| Approval (I-797) | Then: I-485 if in the U.S. and current, or consular processing via NVC. |
| Denial | Options: appeal (I-290B to the AAO), motion to reopen/reconsider, or refile stronger. Refiling is often better than appealing. |

## G. Things that will sink an otherwise good petition

- Describing an **occupation** instead of a **specific proposed endeavor**.
- Recommendation letters that are obviously all written by the same hand. Vary
  structure, voice, length, and detail. Officers notice.
- Letters only from your own supervisors and friends. You need **independent**
  recommenders with no employment or personal relationship to you.
- Claims with no exhibit behind them.
- Copy-pasted "the energy industry is vital to America" argument with no link
  to *your* endeavor.
- Confidential employer material filed without permission.
