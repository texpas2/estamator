# 09 — Sourcing Independent Evidence for Prong 1

> Prong 1 cannot be carried by your own assertions. It needs **independent,
> citable, attachable** sources. Below is exactly what to pull, from where, and
> how to use it. Pull the current edition of each — figures move every year, and
> a stale citation is an easy thing for an officer to discount.
>
> **Rule:** for every source you attach, quote the one sentence you rely on in
> the brief, and highlight it on the exhibit copy. Do not attach a 200-page
> report and hope.

---

## A. Critical-infrastructure designation

| Source | What to pull | Use |
|---|---|---|
| CISA — Critical Infrastructure Sectors (`cisa.gov`) | The Chemical Sector and Energy Sector sector-specific pages | Establishes federal designation of the facilities your endeavor serves |
| PPD-21 / current National Security Memorandum on critical infrastructure | The designation language | Same |
| DOE / DHS Gulf Coast energy infrastructure assessments | Any statement on national dependence on Gulf Coast capacity | Ties your geography to national consequence |

## B. Capacity, economics and national dependence

| Source | What to pull |
|---|---|
| **EIA** (`eia.gov`) — Petroleum & Other Liquids; Natural Gas; Annual Energy Outlook | Gulf Coast (PADD 3) refining capacity share; LNG export capacity and build-out schedule; petrochemical feedstock data |
| **DOE** — Office of Clean Energy Demonstrations; hydrogen hub program pages | If your endeavor touches hydrogen or CCS: the Gulf Coast hub, its schedule and federal cost share |
| **American Chemistry Council** — annual capital investment reports | Announced U.S. chemical capital investment; share on the Gulf Coast; jobs supported per $ of capex |
| **U.S. Census / BEA** | Chemical manufacturing contribution to GDP and exports |
| **GAO** reports on energy infrastructure and federal project cost performance | Independent statements of the cost/schedule problem |

## C. The capital-project performance problem (this is your core prong-1 hook)

| Source | What to pull |
|---|---|
| **Independent Project Analysis (IPA)** published studies | Industry megaproject cost/schedule overrun rates |
| **Construction Industry Institute (CII)**, UT Austin — research summaries | Front-end planning (FEP/FEL) impact on outcomes; modularization research (e.g. RT-283 series) |
| **AACE International** — Recommended Practices (17R-97 cost estimate classification; 18R-97 for process industries) | Establishes that your deliverables are governed by a recognized professional framework, and lets you name your estimate classes precisely |
| **McKinsey Global Institute / Oxford Global Projects** capital projects productivity research | Independent quantification of the productivity gap |

> This is the strongest available framing for a cost/estimating engineer:
> *the problem is documented, national in scale, measured in billions, and
> squarely within your deliverables.* Build § IV.C.2 of the brief on it.

## D. Workforce shortage (feeds prong 3 § B)

| Source | What to pull |
|---|---|
| **BLS Occupational Outlook Handbook** + Employment Projections | Projected openings for {{your SOC code — likely 17-2112 Industrial Engineers, 17-2141 Mechanical Engineers, or 13-1051 Cost Estimators}}; replacement demand |
| **BLS OEWS** | Employment and wage levels in the Houston–The Woodlands–Sugar Land MSA |
| **Associated Builders and Contractors** annual workforce shortage estimate | Gulf Coast craft and staff shortage figures |
| **Construction Users Roundtable / industry craft labor studies** | Gulf Coast labor availability constraints on project execution |
| Employer or association statements of unfilled demand | Direct evidence, if you can obtain a letter |

## E. Process safety and public consequence

| Source | What to pull |
|---|---|
| **U.S. Chemical Safety Board (CSB)** investigation reports — Gulf Coast incidents | Findings tied to design, construction, sequencing or management of change |
| **OSHA** 29 C.F.R. § 1910.119 (PSM) and National Emphasis Program materials | The regulatory frame your facilities operate under |
| **EPA** Risk Management Program, 40 C.F.R. Pt. 68 | Same |
| **OSHA/BLS** injury rates for construction vs. manufacturing settings | Supports a shop-fabrication-displaces-field-hours argument with real numbers |

> Only use this section if your endeavor genuinely bears on safety outcomes and
> you can show the mechanism. A safety claim you cannot connect to your own
> deliverables weakens the brief.

## F. U.S. design content / domestic engineering capability

| Source | What to pull |
|---|---|
| Project documentation showing the U.S. engineering office of record | Drawing title blocks, P.E. stamps, org charts *(redact as needed)* |
| BLS projections for engineering occupations | Domestic capability argument |
| Any applicable federal domestic-content or Buy American provisions relevant to your projects | Only if genuinely applicable |

## G. How to cite in the brief

```
Bad:   "The Gulf Coast is vital to U.S. energy security."
Good:  "PADD 3 accounts for approximately {{X}}% of U.S. operable refining
        capacity (Exhibit 31, U.S. Energy Information Administration,
        {{report title}}, {{date}}, at {{page}}), and {{consequence relevant to
        your endeavor}}."
```

Every prong-1 paragraph should follow that pattern: **independent fact → citation
→ the sentence connecting it to your endeavor.** The third part is the one most
petitions omit, and it is the one the officer is looking for.
