## II. The Legal Standard

Under INA § 203(b)(2)(B)(i), the Secretary may waive the job offer requirement —
and thus the labor certification — for an EB-2 beneficiary when it is in the
national interest. The governing framework is *Matter of Dhanasar*, 26 I&N Dec.
884 (AAO 2016), which superseded *Matter of New York State Department of
Transportation*, 22 I&N Dec. 215 (Acting Assoc. Comm'r 1998). USCIS must grant
the waiver where the petitioner demonstrates by a **preponderance of the
evidence** that:

1. the proposed endeavor has both **substantial merit and national importance**;
2. the petitioner is **well positioned to advance** the proposed endeavor; and
3. **on balance**, it would be beneficial to the United States to waive the job
   offer and labor certification requirements.

*Dhanasar*, 26 I&N Dec. at 889. The standard of proof is preponderance —
"more likely than not" — not clear and convincing evidence, and not certainty of
future success. *Matter of Chawathe*, 25 I&N Dec. 369, 375–76 (AAO 2010).

Two points from *Dhanasar* and the USCIS Policy Manual (Vol. 6, Pt. F, Ch. 5)
bear directly on this petition:

- **National importance turns on the endeavor's prospective impact, not on
  geographic scope alone.** *Dhanasar*, 26 I&N Dec. at 889. An endeavor executed
  at specific sites may have national importance where its effects — on national
  infrastructure capacity, on an industry's methods, on public safety, or on the
  economy — extend beyond those sites.
- **Prong 2 does not require a guarantee of success.** The question is whether
  the petitioner's education, skills, record of success, and plan make them well
  positioned to advance the endeavor. *Id.* at 890.
